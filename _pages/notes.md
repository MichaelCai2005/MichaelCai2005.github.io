## Columbia University

### Quantum Optimization and Machine Learning (ELEN 4730)

- **Files:** [PDF](</assets/pdf/notes/quantum-notes%20(2).pdf>) [PDF](/assets/pdf/ELEN4730%20my%20notes.pdf)
- **Description:** Notes taken (live) from CU's ELEN 4730. The course is a topics course in quantum optimization and machine learning, and uses Xiaodong Wang's _Lecture Notes on Quantum Optimization and Machine Learning_ as the primary reference.
- **Topics Covered:** Quantum algorithms, Deutsch-Jozsa, Bernstein-Vazirani, Simon's Algorithm, Quantum Fourier Transform, Quantum Phase Estimation, Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization, Grover Adaptive Search, Quantum Annealing, Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm, High-order binary optimization, Variational Quantum Eigensolver, Quantum Neural Network, Quantum Support Vector Machines
- **Pre-requisites:** Linear algebra
- **Remark:** Being a freshman, I was told that the prerequisites were linear algebra. I believe this was not the case. Additionally, the notes are by Kevin Liu as my notes presented were one of the first times typing in latex.

### Introduction to Numerical Methods (APMA 4300)

- **Files:** [PDF](/assets/pdf/notes/APMA4300.pdf) [TeX Source (GitHub)](https://github.com/MichaelCai2005/APMA4300-Notes/blob/main/APMA4300.pdf)
- **Description:** Notes taken (live) from CU's APMA 4300. The course is a introductory course on numerical methods used in linear algebra and ordinary differential equations.
- **Topics Covered:** Elementary numerical linear algebra, least-square methods, nonlinear equations and optimization, polynomial interpolation, numerical differentiation and integration, initial-value and boundary problems for ordinary differential equations and systems, eigenvalue problems
- **Pre-requisites:** Students are expected to have a strong background in multi-variable calculus (on the level of APMA E2001 and above), linear algebra (on the level of APMA E3101 and above), ordinary differential equations (on the level of APMA E2101 and above), and mathematical analysis (on the level of MATH UN2500 or GU4601). Programming experience in Python (or equivalence) is required

### Introduction to Quantum Computing (COMSW 4281)

- **Files:** [PDF](/assets/pdf/notes/COMSW%204281.pdf)
- **Description:** Notes taken (live) from CU's COMSW 4281. The course is a topics introductory course on Quantum Computing, and uses Henry Yuen's _Lecture Slides on Quantum Computing_ as the primary reference.
- **Topics Covered:** The fundamental postulates of quantum information theory, Entanglement and nonlocality, The quantum circuit model, Basic quantum protocols such as quantum teleportation and superdense coding, Basic quantum algorithms such as Simon’s algorithm, the Quantum Fourier Transform, Phase Estimation, Shor’s Factoring algorithm, Grover search, amplitude amplification, Quantum error correction and fault-tolerance, Quantum cryptography, quantum advantage/quantum supremacy, quantum complexity theory
- **Pre-requisites:** Linear algebra, Quantum mechanics/information, and Analysis of algorithms
- **Remark:** These notes are incomplete as lecture had slides provided.

### Advanced Mechanics (PHYS 4003)

- **Files:** [PDF](/assets/pdf/notes/PHYS4003.pdf) - [TeX Source (GitHub)](https://github.com/MichaelCai2005/PHYS4003-Notes/blob/main/PHYS4003.pdf)
- **Description:** Notes taken (live) from CU's PHYS 4003. This course goes over the 2nd edition of Goldstein's Classical Mechanics.
- **Topics Covered:** Constraints, holonomic and non-holonomic, Virtual work, D'Alembert's principle, The Lagrangian and Euler-Lagrange equations, Lagrange multipliers, Generalized momenta and forces, cyclical coordinates, Inclusion of electric and magnetic forces into Lagrangian, gauge transformations, Calculus of variations, principle of least action, Point transformations and classical Noether's theorem, Central forces, effective potential, virial theorem, Euler angles, rotations, angular momentum, Legendre Transformations, generalized momenta, Hamiltonian, Hamiltonian and Alternative action principles, Hamilton's equations, Poisson brackets, Symplectic geometry, phase space, phase portraits, Canonical transformations, infinitesimal transformations and canonical invariants, Hamilton's principle function, Hamilton-Jacobi equation.
- **Pre-requisites:** second-year course of classical mechanics
