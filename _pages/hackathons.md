These are the short term projects I have worked on with other undergraduate students:

### MIT iQuHack: Hamiltonian and Linbldadian Simulation

- **Files:** [GitHub](https://github.com/Rohan-T144/2025-Quantinuum/) [Report](</assets/pdf/hackathons/Report,%20Collapse_Now__Fix_Later%20(2).pdf>)
- **Description:** MIT Quantum Hackathon 3rd Place: Utilized Quantinuum’s Pytket to construct XXZ chain and transverse field Ising Hamiltonians. Simulated first and second order trotter decomposition algorithms and simulated energy simulation over time; implemented dilation operators for dissipative dynamics for linbladian simulations; innovated an automated optimizer that finds the optimal number of trotter steps to account for accuracy and circuit complexity using Lagrange multipliers and objective function

### New York City Quantum Hackathon: VQE Portfolio Optimization

- **Files:** [GitHub](https://github.com/Lion-Q/finance-qbraid) [Slides](/assets/pdf/hackathons/Team%203_%20Lion-Q%20Slide%20Deck%20Final.pptx.pdf) [Report](</assets/pdf/hackathons/Lion_Q%20Paper%20(2).pdf>)
- **Description:** NYC Quantum Hackathon 1st Place: We developed a QUBO formulation for VQE optimization based on Hodson et al.'s paper. We utilized IBM Qiskit Portfolio Optimization Version 0.6.1 to assess gradient performance through Finite Difference, SPSA, Analytic, and Natural methods while also evaluating ansatz performance using SU2, ZZ, and TwoLocal configurations in linear, full, and cyclic formations. Additionally, we conducted baseline portfolio optimization using Python by implementing Monte Carlo simulations, maximum diversification strategies, and a minimum eigen solver approach.

## Yale Hackathon: Slide Forge

- **Files:** [GitHub](https://github.com/Samuel-O-M/YHack-2024)
- **Description:** Yale Hackathon 1st Place: SlideForge is an application designed by researchers for researchers. It enables professionals such as professors and graduate students in academia to convert their LaTeX-formatted papers into presentation slides with ease. Our goal is to help researchers build their community, save time, and enhance collaboration and reputation between academia and the public.

## Stanford Treehacks: Codeforge Pages

- **Files:** [GitHub](https://github.com/KshitijTeotia06/frontendTreeHacks)
- **Description:** CodeForge Pages asks users to enter information about their company along with the name of the company. Based on this information, CodeForge generates a multi–component landing page in React that contains beautiful layout, text, and style. Users can view the rendered code in either the web-view or a simulated mobile view. When pleased with the website, they can export it–which will download a zip of all the react components. All users are also authenticated – meaning when you create a project, we associate it with your profile and it can be accessed at any time through the dashboard.
