Intermediate Physics Lab (PHYS 3081)

  • Files: Report

  • Description: This experiment aimed to measure and compare the decay times and light yields of three scintillating crystals: BGO, CsI(Tl), and LYSO(Ce). Using a Cs-137 source to excite the crystals, the emitted photons were detected by a Silicon Photomultiplier (SiPM), and the signals were digitized using an ADC unit. We calculated the average waveform and light yield for each crystal. The measured decay times were 67.75 ns for LYSO(Ce), 307.32 ns for BGO, and 935.14 ns for CsI(Tl). These results were compared with accepted values of 40 ns for LYSO(Ce), 300 ns for BGO, and 1000 ns for CsI(Tl), showing de- viations of approximately 69.38%, 2.44%, and 6.48%, respectively [1]. Experimental limitations, such as equipment constraints and signal processing methods, likely contributed to the discrepancies observed. Improved alignment and analysis techniques led to more accurate mea- surements compared to initial results.

  • Files: Report
  • Description: Silicon Photomultiplier tubes are important in multiple areas of medical and particle physics to detect minute amounts of photons. The SiPM converts analog signals of light to digital signals as volt- age and produces a gain similar to that of a Photomultiplier Tube (PMT). Multiple characteristics effect the efficiency and accuracy of a SiPM, notably dark count, optical cross talk, and after pulsing. In this experiment, we use the SP5601 LED Driver, SP5600 PSAU, and DT5702 Digitzer to detect possible errors and determine the gain. Sig- nificant challenges, such as the accurate measurement of dark counts arising from thermal excitation [1], optical cross-talk where avalanches in one microcell triggered adjacent cells, and photon count to standard deviation curve, were addressed and measured. The results demon- strate that while significant noise must be managed, SiPMs success- fully achieve expected gain values of 2.1 · 106 consistent with theo- retical predictions and other studies. We additionally saw a lower optical cross talk (OCT) value of 5.6% compared to the 10-20 and a higher dark count rate (DCR) of 1.08 M Hz mm2 compared to 0.5 M Hz mm2 . This experiment provides valuable insights into noise management techniques, necessary for optimizing SiPMs for applications in dark matter physics, quantum physics, and medical imaging.
